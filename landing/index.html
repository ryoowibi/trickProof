PAY-ONLY KEY01 / 04

One method. That's it.

Budi's NEAR access key is scoped to the vault and the single method pay. set_policy is refused by the protocol before any contract runs.

RULES IN STATE02 / 04

Limits it can't touch.

Per-transaction cap, daily cap and allowlist live in contract state. Policy methods check the caller and only accept the owner.

OWNER APPROVAL03 / 04

Strangers wait at the door.

Within the limits, a payment to anyone outside the allowlist is held. The owner approves or rejects it from a separate process.

ON-CHAIN RECORD04 / 04

Every attempt, on the record.

Rejected payments return a reason instead of panicking, so the log entry is committed. The vault keeps its last 100 decisions.

06 Objections

Questions

Short answers about what Trickproof does, and what it doesn't.

Read the README →
Does Trickproof stop prompt injection?
No, and it doesn't try to. Budi is fooled on purpose. Trickproof limits the damage: a tricked agent can spend at most the daily limit at allowlisted receivers, and everything else is rejected or held for the owner.
Why can't the agent change the rules?
Two independent reasons. Its NEAR access key only allows pay, so the protocol refuses any other method. And the contract checks the caller, accepting only the owner for policy changes.
What happened in the Grok and Bankrbot case?
In May 2026 an X user asked Grok to decode Morse code; Grok's reply tagged Bankrbot, which executed it as a transfer of about 3 billion DRB (reported $150K–$200K). Sources: OECD.AI, Giskard. A Cornell Tech and Yale study also found 9 of 10 models raised their own wallet cap (coverage).
How is this different from OutLayer?
OutLayer offers agent custody with spending limits, whitelists and approvals enforced inside a TEE before signing (docs). Trickproof keeps the policy in ordinary NEAR contract state, enforced by consensus with a native scoped access key, and every rejected attempt is a public on-chain record. They're complementary; Trickproof isn't integrated with OutLayer.
Why log rejections instead of failing?
On NEAR, a panic rolls back every state change, including the log entry. Returning a rejection keeps each attempt visible on-chain.
Is it production-ready?
No. Testnet only, not audited, native NEAR only. The README lists the known limitations.
